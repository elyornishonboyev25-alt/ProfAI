# Full mock section introductions

Each section begins with an original 1280×720 instructional video in the ProfAI IELTS design. Listening and Reading record the actual `IELTSReadingInterface`, using the live catalog and its native controls. British English Kokoro narration uses natural sentence prosody, short pauses and gentle fades, without accelerating the recorded speech. Duration follows the spoken instructions; there is no 24/25-second limit. `fullMockIntroMedia.json` records the actual audio-derived duration and chapter boundaries, so captions, chapter links and the player clock follow the prerecorded speech.

Listening briefly explains all four recording contexts; Academic Reading explains all three passages without claiming that particular question types belong to a fixed passage. The focus is recorded cursor demonstrations of selecting text, adding/deleting highlights, changing text size and contrast, attaching notes, opening separate scratch notes, navigating parts/questions, flagging questions, reviewing answers, watching the timer, and Submit → Cancel / Submit test → the next skill. Every demonstration has corresponding English narration. The recording fixture uses existing tests in an isolated browser profile; it never adds catalog content or writes user attempts. Frame capture requests run on unthrottled intervals, including background recording tabs, so chapters contain motion rather than still images.

The introduction has a candidate header, section tabs, information panel, headphones/audio check, navigable video chapters, playback controls, synchronized written captions, full transcript, **Skip video & start**, and **Start Test**. Native video completion automatically starts the section exactly once. Pausing or seeking chapters does not start it; the start/skip controls remain available before completion. Autoplay restrictions expose **Enable sound**; failed video retains written instructions and Start Test. Browser cache versioning prevents older videos from being reused.

Full Mock **Open** requests fullscreen on the document in the click gesture and opens the first unfinished section directly. Completed mocks open their results overview. Fullscreen persists across routes; entry controls remain available when the browser blocks it or the candidate exits. Unsupported browsers retain the immersive layout.

`FullMockSectionGate` keeps runners unmounted during instructions: no test audio, timer or microphone starts. Starting is stored per account, mock and section. Full mocks bypass Practice/Simulation selection and the launch delay, always using the complete exam paper and its normal timing. Fresh Listening attempts start their audio automatically; a saved Listening session still resumes its original track/position through the existing playback recovery control. Writing starts its timed tasks immediately, including StrictMode. Speaking automatically requests microphone access and starts its examiner session after permission is granted; denied permission or blocked examiner playback keeps the existing recovery controls. Reloading preserves existing deadlines, including legacy active Reading/Listening and Writing sessions. Individual practice and saved-attempt review retain their existing modes and bypass introductions. Stale routes still redirect to the first unfinished section.

Listening's existing countdown starts only after the final test recording, lasts two minutes, persists its original deadline on reload and automatically submits at expiry. Earlier tracks, instruction videos and review playback do not start it. Reading, Writing and Speaking timing is unchanged.

Completed catalog cards show **Retake** beside **Review** without changing their dimensions. Retake archives the previous four-section result in account storage, resets only the chosen mock's progress, intro gates, objective sessions and Writing draft/deadline, then opens Listening instructions. Other mocks, accounts and standalone practice sessions remain intact. The results page retains the overall score above four keyboard-accessible section cards; selecting a card shows its band, objective correct answers/accuracy, or Writing/Speaking responses and feedback.

## Official references and scope

Checked on 2026-10-12 against the [official IDP computer-test startup guidance](https://ielts.idp.com/turkey/prepare/article-how-computer-delivered-ielts-works/en-gb), the [IDP familiarisation tests](https://ielts.idp.com/about/ielts-familiarisation-tests), and the live [official Academic Reading demo](https://demo-ielts.inspera.com/player/?assessmentRunId=131013388&context=exam#/section/128121930/question/128121896/scorableItem/1). The latter uses a flat header, part instructions, passage/questions side by side and bottom part/question navigation. Our tutorial demonstrates the controls available in `IELTSReadingInterface.tsx`, including the **Highlight**, **Note**, **Delete highlight**, **Display settings**, **Text size**, **Contrast**, **Notes**, **Review**, and flag controls.

Part descriptions follow [IELTS Academic Listening](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening) and [IELTS Academic Reading](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading). Real Speaking uses a human examiner; the video explicitly identifies ProfAI's AI practice examiner. These are original ProfAI instructional videos, not official IELTS media or a certified replica of every test-centre implementation.

Existing overall scoring averages the four bands and rounds to the nearest half band, with .25/.75 rounding upwards; Writing weights Task 2 twice as heavily. Regression checks retain official worked examples. Raw-score conversion can vary slightly by test version. AI Writing/Speaking bands remain practice estimates rather than certified examiner scores.

## Narration and regeneration

Narration uses [Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M), its British English `bf_emma` voice and the MIT [kokoro-onnx](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx) inference package. The model is Apache-2.0 licensed. This is synthetic narration, disclosed in the instruction panel. It needs no website runtime API key or external video embed.

1. Create an isolated Python 3.12 environment in ignored `tmp/kokoro-runtime/venv`; install `kokoro-onnx==0.6.1`, `onnxruntime==1.20.1` and `soundfile==0.14.0`. Download the upstream `kokoro-v1.0.onnx` and `voices-v1.0.bin` into `tmp/kokoro-runtime/model`. On Windows, supply the Microsoft Visual C++ runtime; a portable official VCLibs package may be extracted into `tmp/kokoro-runtime/vclibs`. No environment, DLLs or model binaries belong in the Git commit.
2. Run `node scripts/capture-ielts-intro-screens.mjs` with Microsoft Edge installed to capture native screens and cursor gestures into ignored `tmp/ielts-intro-screens`. Cursor gestures have continuous paths with easing only at their start/end; selection drags have steady velocity. The renderer evaluates these paths independently of screenshot timing at 60 fps, avoiding pauses at every screenshot boundary. Then run `node scripts/generate-ielts-intros.mjs`. Override `IELTS_INTRO_PYTHON`, `IELTS_INTRO_KOKORO_DIR`, `IELTS_INTRO_DLL_DIR`, `IELTS_INTRO_SCREEN_DIR`, or `IELTS_INTRO_BROWSER` for other local paths. Narration is synthesized at speed 0.96; audio playback rate is **1**.
3. The generator exports sections sequentially so encoders do not compete for cursor frames, checks average rendering cadence, writes the four intentional product videos under `public/media/ielts-intros`, and writes timing/motion metadata. `IELTS_INTRO_VOICE_DIR` can resume an interrupted working directory with unchanged copy; omit it for a clean regeneration after editing narration.

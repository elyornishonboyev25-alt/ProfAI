# IELTS vocabulary review — 7 October 2026

Reviewed all 30 Listening and 30 Reading Full Test vocabulary sets against the current visible catalog, including the regrouped Reading roadmap tests. Replaced 916 of the 1,950 Listening/Reading entries and added 232 source-specific meanings to the shared lexicon.

- Listening retains 20 words per test and coverage of all four parts.
- Reading retains 15 words per passage, across three passages per test.
- Each selected word occurs in its attributed passage or visible question text. Answer keys, explanations, IDs and audio metadata are excluded from source checks.
- Existing Writing/Speaking sets, existing lexicon meanings, set identities and retained word positions are preserved. Vocabulary XP and activity rewards are unchanged.

Selection favours advanced academic vocabulary and specialised terms, using the [Oxford 3000/5000 level reference](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000) as a guide. Technical terms and inflected forms require contextual judgment; this review does not assign blanket C1/C2 certification. Some short Listening sources contain fewer advanced terms than the existing 20-word set requires, so their strongest available source words are retained to preserve both source accuracy and word counts.

The offline translation catalog covers every current IELTS, SAT and Article vocabulary entry, plus legacy IELTS terms for saved words. Existing Uzbek translations take precedence. Ambiguous Russian meanings were checked against the English definitions; multi-sense words include appropriate alternatives. Explicit translations stored with a personal word take precedence over the catalog.

Validation checks source identity, visible source occurrence, counts, distinct meanings, both translation languages, saved-word persistence and unchanged XP rules. UI checks cover selection across routes, flashcards, saved words, preference persistence and restricted storage.
